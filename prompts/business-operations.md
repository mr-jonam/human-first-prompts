# Business & Operations

Prompts for clearer processes, decisions, and coordination. Generated material is a working draft, not legal, financial, compliance, or executive approval.

## 1. Draft a usable standard operating procedure

**Use when:** a recurring process lives mainly in someone's memory.

```text
Turn these process notes into a draft SOP.

Purpose and trigger: [details]
People and systems involved: [details]
Current steps and exceptions: [paste]
Controls, approvals, and evidence: [details]
Known failure modes: [list]

Return scope, prerequisites, roles, numbered steps, decision points, exceptions, records to retain, escalation path, and review cadence. Distinguish confirmed steps from assumptions and questions. Add a short walkthrough scenario for validation.

Do not invent policy, permissions, system behavior, or regulatory requirements.
```

**Human check:** have the people who perform and own the process run a real example against the draft.

## 2. Prepare a project kickoff that exposes ambiguity

**Use when:** a project is approved in principle but not yet aligned in practice.

```text
Create a project kickoff brief from these inputs: [paste]

Sponsor and accountable owner: [roles]
Desired outcome and deadline: [details]
Known scope, constraints, and dependencies: [details]
Stakeholders and affected users: [list]

Draft an outcome statement, in-scope and out-of-scope lists, success evidence, roles, decision rights, milestones, assumptions, dependencies, risks, and unresolved questions. Propose a 60-minute kickoff agenda focused on decisions rather than presentations.

Flag conflicting inputs instead of harmonizing them silently.
```

**Human check:** the sponsor and delivery owners explicitly confirm scope, decision rights, and success evidence.

## 3. Critique an objective and its key results

**Use when:** an OKR risks rewarding activity, gaming, or goals outside the team's control.

```text
Review this draft objective and key results: [paste]

Team mandate and strategy: [details]
Baseline and available data: [details]
Important guardrails: [quality, safety, people, cost]

Assess clarity, strategic relevance, controllability, measurability, incentives, and possible harmful optimization. Rewrite only where needed. Separate outcomes from initiatives, state the baseline, and add guardrail indicators and evidence owners. Identify what should remain a health metric rather than a target.

Do not manufacture baselines or imply that a metric proves causation.
```

**Human check:** leadership and the people doing the work agree that the measures encourage the intended behavior.

## 4. Compare vendors without hiding trade-offs

**Use when:** proposals need a transparent, auditable comparison.

```text
Build a vendor comparison from the supplied evidence.

Decision context and must-haves: [details]
Weighted criteria approved by the team: [list]
Vendor proposals, demos, references, and terms: [paste or summarize]
Budget and switching constraints: [details]

Create a matrix containing evidence, source, confidence, gaps, total cost categories, implementation risk, security/privacy questions, exit conditions, and reference checks. Show sensitivity if weights change. List contract or technical claims requiring specialist review.

Do not infer absent features, score missing data as zero, or make the final procurement decision.
```

**Human check:** procurement, security, legal, technical, and business owners validate their criteria and evidence.

## 5. Build a living risk register

**Use when:** a team needs explicit ownership and mitigation, not a ceremonial risk list.

```text
Turn these project notes into a draft risk register: [paste]

Risk scale and organizational definitions: [details]
Decision horizon: [date or phase]
Known dependencies and assumptions: [details]

For each risk, state cause, uncertain event, consequence, existing controls, likelihood and impact using only the supplied scale, owner, trigger, mitigation, contingency, due date, and residual uncertainty. Separate risks, issues, assumptions, and dependencies. Flag correlated risks and missing owners.

Do not invent probabilities or label a risk accepted without an accountable decision.
```

**Human check:** owners confirm the risks, actions, triggers, and acceptance authority at a real review.

## 6. Draft a customer-support response with a safe escalation

**Use when:** a recurring support situation needs consistency without silencing judgment.

```text
Draft a support response and agent guidance.

Customer message: [paste]
Verified account or product facts: [details]
Relevant approved policy: [paste or link]
Actions the agent may take: [list]
Escalation triggers: [list]
Tone requirements: [details]

Return a customer-facing reply, internal next steps, facts to verify, and escalation conditions. Acknowledge impact without admitting unsupported facts. Explain limits plainly and offer only available remedies. Mark placeholders for account-specific information.

Do not expose internal data, invent policy, blame the customer, or promise an outcome outside authority.
```

**Human check:** an authorized support owner validates policy, remedy, privacy, and account details.

## 7. Prepare a capacity conversation from incomplete data

**Use when:** demand, staffing, and deadlines appear incompatible.

```text
Organize these inputs for a capacity-planning conversation.

Demand and deadlines: [details]
Available people or resources: [details]
Historical throughput and variability: [data]
Fixed obligations and constraints: [details]
Unknowns: [list]

Create low, expected, and high-demand scenarios with explicit assumptions. Identify bottlenecks, skills constraints, queue effects, and work that can be delayed, reduced, automated, or stopped. Provide decision options with service, cost, people, and risk trade-offs.

Do not treat people as interchangeable units or present estimates as commitments.
```

**Human check:** resource owners and affected teams review assumptions, sustainable workload, and priorities.

## 8. Analyze a process before automating it

**Use when:** automation is proposed but the underlying work may be unclear or wasteful.

```text
Map this process and identify improvement opportunities before proposing automation.

Process purpose and customer: [details]
Observed steps, handoffs, waits, rework, and exceptions: [paste]
Volumes and timing evidence: [data]
Constraints and control requirements: [details]

Return a current-state map, value-adding and non-value-adding steps, root-cause hypotheses, missing observations, and small experiments. For automation candidates, state prerequisites, failure handling, human override, monitoring, and risks. Include a do-nothing option.

Do not automate a policy decision or control without the accountable experts.
```

**Human check:** observe the real process and involve the people affected before changing roles or controls.

## 9. Structure a business proposal around evidence

**Use when:** an initiative needs a decision-ready case rather than promotional prose.

```text
Draft a business proposal from these materials: [paste]

Decision requested and owner: [details]
Problem evidence and affected groups: [details]
Options considered: [list]
Costs, benefits, dependencies, and risks: [known facts]

Produce an executive summary, current state, options including status quo, assumptions, qualitative and quantitative impacts, implementation outline, risks, decision criteria, and recommendation with confidence. Mark unsupported numbers [VALIDATE] and show which assumptions drive the conclusion.

Do not fabricate ROI, certainty, approvals, or legal and financial conclusions.
```

**Human check:** finance, legal, delivery, and accountable business owners validate the relevant claims before approval.

## 10. Turn operating data into a decision update

**Use when:** leaders need a concise update that preserves bad news and uncertainty.

```text
Create a decision-oriented operating update from this data: [paste]

Audience and reporting period: [details]
Prior commitments and thresholds: [details]
Decisions or support needed: [list]

Return: headline outcome, metric table with source and comparison, what changed, drivers supported by evidence, exceptions, risks, actions and owners, decisions needed, and an appendix for detail. Separate observed correlation from causal explanation. Preserve unfavorable results and note data-quality limits.

Do not cherry-pick metrics, hide missed commitments, or invent a recovery forecast.
```

**Human check:** metric owners verify figures and accountable leaders make the requested decisions.
