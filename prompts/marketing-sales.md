# Marketing & Sales

Prompts for ethical, evidence-aware growth work. They help create drafts and experiments without inventing customers, results, testimonials, or urgency.

## 1. Clarify positioning from real customer evidence

**Use when:** a product sounds generic or the team disagrees about its strongest value.

```text
Turn the supplied evidence into a positioning brief.

Product and category: [details]
Best-fit customers: [details]
Interview notes, reviews, win/loss notes, or usage data: [paste]
Competitors or alternatives: [list]
Claims we can prove: [evidence]

Separate customer language, observed patterns, hypotheses, and unsupported claims. Draft: target situation, costly problem, differentiated outcome, reasons to believe, alternatives, and a one-sentence positioning statement. Provide three variants with different emphasis and a testable assumption behind each.

Do not invent market research, customer quotes, superiority, or performance numbers.
```

**Human check:** validate the strongest variant with actual customers and the people responsible for product truth.

## 2. Synthesize customer interviews without erasing disagreement

**Use when:** several interviews need to become useful themes rather than a persuasive anecdote.

```text
Analyze these customer interview notes: [paste]

Research question: [question]
Participant context and known sampling limits: [details]

Create a theme table with supporting excerpts, participant count, counterexamples, confidence, and unanswered questions. Distinguish needs, behaviors, workarounds, desired outcomes, and solution requests. Highlight contradictions and segments that may behave differently.

Do not imply statistical representativeness or combine different speakers into a fabricated quote. End with the three next interviews or data checks that would reduce uncertainty most.
```

**Human check:** return to recordings or notes before using any quotation or making a product decision.

## 3. Build a sustainable content calendar

**Use when:** content needs a coherent purpose and a realistic publishing rhythm.

```text
Design a [number]-week content calendar.

Audience and problem: [details]
Business objective: [objective]
Channels: [list]
Available expertise and source material: [details]
Publishing capacity: [hours or people]
Important dates and constraints: [details]

Propose recurring themes, one primary item per cycle, sensible derivatives, owner, evidence source, call to action, and success signal. Balance education, proof, perspective, and conversation. Mark any topic requiring legal, technical, or customer approval.

Avoid filler, fake trends, and a cadence that exceeds the stated capacity.
```

**Human check:** confirm owners and source material before committing to dates.

## 4. Draft a newsletter people can scan and trust

**Use when:** turning updates or expertise into a useful subscriber email.

```text
Draft a newsletter from only the material below.

Audience: [who]
Desired reader outcome: [outcome]
Source notes and links: [paste]
Voice examples: [optional]
Call to action: [action]
Length limit: [limit]

Return five subject lines, preview text, a skimmable draft, and a plain-text version. Put the most useful idea first, support claims with supplied sources, and label anything that needs verification. Use one clear call to action and avoid manufactured scarcity or clickbait.
```

**Human check:** test every link, verify claims, and review consent and unsubscribe requirements before sending.

## 5. Adapt one idea across social channels

**Use when:** repurposing content without publishing identical, context-free posts everywhere.

```text
Adapt this source idea for [LinkedIn / Instagram / X / TikTok / other]: [paste]

Audience: [details]
Goal: [goal]
Brand voice: [examples or traits]
Assets available: [list]
Constraints and claims policy: [details]

For each selected channel, propose a native format, opening, concise draft, visual or interaction idea, and meaningful response prompt. Explain what changed for the channel. Keep factual claims traceable and provide accessible alt text where relevant.

Do not fabricate engagement, testimonials, trends, or personal experience.
```

**Human check:** review platform rules, accessibility, context, and tone in the actual publishing interface.

## 6. Create a landing-page evidence map

**Use when:** a landing page needs a clear argument before polished copy.

```text
Build an evidence map and draft structure for this landing page.

Offer: [details]
Visitor situation and intent: [details]
Desired action: [action]
Verified benefits and proof: [evidence]
Known objections: [list]
Restrictions or required disclosures: [details]

Map each proposed claim to evidence, likely objection, and page section. Then draft a headline, subhead, section outline, calls to action, and an FAQ. Mark weak claims [EVIDENCE NEEDED] and offer a safer alternative wording.

Do not add testimonials, guarantees, customer logos, or urgency that was not supplied.
```

**Human check:** product, legal, and customer-facing owners must approve claims and disclosures.

## 7. Prepare ethical cold outreach

**Use when:** contacting a relevant prospect with a specific, honest reason.

```text
Draft a short outreach sequence based on legitimate relevance.

Recipient role and organization: [details]
Verified trigger or problem: [source]
What we offer and can prove: [details]
Why this may matter now: [evidence]
Desired next step: [small request]
Jurisdiction or contact-policy constraints: [details]

Write an initial message and two respectful follow-ups. State the relevance early, separate observation from inference, keep personalization factual, and make opting out easy. Include a version that asks for direction instead of a meeting.

Do not pretend familiarity, scrape sensitive facts, or create false urgency.
```

**Human check:** confirm lawful basis, contact policy, factual personalization, and suppression lists before sending.

## 8. Prepare a discovery call without scripting the buyer

**Use when:** a sales conversation should uncover fit rather than force a pitch.

```text
Create a discovery-call guide.

Account and participant context: [verified facts]
Our fit criteria and disqualifiers: [details]
Hypotheses to test: [list]
Known constraints: [details]

Provide an opening contract, questions about current process, impact, stakeholders, alternatives, timing, and decision criteria. Add follow-up probes, signals of poor fit, and a note template separating facts, quotes, interpretations, and commitments. Finish with mutually useful next-step options.

Do not assume pain, budget, authority, or intent; do not coach manipulation.
```

**Human check:** listen for the buyer's actual language and accept a clear no or no-fit outcome.

## 9. Turn verified results into a case study

**Use when:** customer work deserves a credible story without inflated attribution.

```text
Draft a case study from these approved materials: [paste]

Audience and use: [details]
Customer-approved claims and quotes: [list]
Baseline, intervention, and result evidence: [details]
Other factors that influenced the result: [details]

Create a concise narrative covering context, challenge, selection criteria, implementation, result, limitations, and lessons. Attribute shared work accurately. Flag every statement requiring customer approval or stronger evidence, and provide a version without confidential metrics.

Do not invent causality, quotes, dates, or customer enthusiasm.
```

**Human check:** obtain explicit customer approval for wording, identity, quotations, and data before publication.

## 10. Design a measurable campaign experiment

**Use when:** a campaign idea needs a falsifiable test instead of a vanity-metric forecast.

```text
Turn this campaign idea into a bounded experiment: [idea]

Audience and funnel stage: [details]
Business outcome: [outcome]
Baseline evidence: [data]
Budget, time, and channels: [constraints]
Brand, privacy, and legal limits: [details]

Define one hypothesis, primary metric, guardrail metrics, audience, comparison, minimum useful duration, decision rule, and instrumentation checks. Identify confounders and what the experiment cannot prove. Provide a pre-launch checklist and a neutral result-report template.

Do not promise lift or reverse-engineer a success threshold after results arrive.
```

**Human check:** analytics and channel owners validate measurement, consent, budget, and stopping rules.
