# Work & Career

Prompts for clearer preparation and self-reflection. Never fabricate achievements, credentials, or experience.

## 1. Build a meeting brief that earns its time

**Use when:** a meeting needs a decision, not another round of status updates.

```text
Turn these notes into a decision-focused meeting brief.

Meeting purpose: [purpose]
People attending and their roles: [list]
Decision owner: [person or role]
Decision or outcome needed: [outcome]
Known facts and links: [notes]
Open questions or disagreements: [list]
Time available: [minutes]

Return a one-page brief with: desired outcome, pre-read facts, decisions required, options and trade-offs, a timed agenda, and named follow-ups. Put information-only updates in an appendix. Flag any item that lacks an owner or evidence.

Do not resolve disagreements by inventing consensus. If a meeting is unnecessary, suggest an asynchronous alternative and explain why.
```

**Human check:** have the decision owner confirm the question and options before sending the invitation.

## 2. Turn work notes into evidence-based achievements

**Use when:** preparing a review, portfolio, or résumé from messy notes.

```text
Help me extract truthful achievements from these work notes:
[paste notes]

Target use: [performance review / portfolio / résumé / interview]
Target role or competency framework: [details]

For each potential achievement, separate:
- situation or problem
- my specific action
- collaborators and their contribution
- observable result
- evidence I have
- missing metric or context to verify

Then draft concise achievement statements using only supported claims. Where a number is missing, insert [VERIFY METRIC] rather than estimating it. Avoid inflated verbs, false ownership, and generic praise. Suggest questions I can answer to strengthen the evidence.
```

**Human check:** confirm every claim with records or colleagues and represent shared work fairly.

## 3. Prepare interview stories without scripting a persona

**Use when:** you need memorable examples that remain honest and conversational.

```text
Act as an interview coach. The role is [role] at [organization or type]. The interview will assess [competencies].

My real experience notes: [paste]
Areas I am worried about: [list]

Map my strongest real examples to the competencies. For each, create a flexible story outline with context, challenge, my actions, other people's roles, outcome, and what I learned. Add likely follow-up questions and any claim an interviewer may challenge.

Do not invent projects, metrics, tools, or leadership. If the evidence is weak, say so and help me frame an honest adjacent example. Finish with five questions that test whether this role and team fit me too.
```

**Human check:** practice from keywords, not a memorized script, and remove anything you could not defend in detail.

## 4. Convert feedback into a bounded growth plan

**Use when:** feedback is emotionally charged, vague, or hard to act on.

```text
Help me process this workplace feedback without assuming it is fully correct or fully wrong.

Feedback received: [exact words or close notes]
Context and relationship: [details]
My initial reaction: [reaction]
Examples I know about: [facts]
Constraints or systemic factors: [details]

Separate observable behavior, interpretation, impact, and missing evidence. Propose clarifying questions that are calm and specific. Then draft a 30-day experiment with one behavior to practice, one signal to track, and one check-in with the feedback giver.

Also identify what should not be individualized if it may reflect unclear expectations, workload, discrimination, or a process problem. Do not diagnose motives or advise retaliation.
```

**Human check:** discuss consequential or potentially discriminatory feedback with a trusted manager, representative, HR professional, or adviser as appropriate.
