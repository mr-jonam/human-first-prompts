# Personal Life

Practical prompts for planning and everyday choices. They organize your thinking; they do not make medical, legal, or financial decisions for you.

## 1. Plan a realistic week from actual capacity

**Use when:** your list is longer than your available time and everything feels equally urgent.

```text
Act as a practical planning partner. Help me design a realistic week, not an aspirational one.

My fixed commitments: [days, times, travel]
My available energy by day: [low / medium / high]
Tasks and rough deadlines: [list]
What matters most this week: [one or two outcomes]
What I am willing to postpone: [list or "help me decide"]

First identify missing time costs, dependencies, and tasks that are too vague to schedule. Then produce:
1. A weekly plan with no more than three priority blocks per day
2. Buffer time and one deliberately unscheduled block
3. A "not this week" list with reasons
4. A minimum viable version for any overloaded day
5. The two assumptions most likely to break the plan

Do not optimize every minute. If the commitments cannot fit, say so plainly and propose trade-offs instead of compressing them unrealistically.
```

**Human check:** confirm durations against your calendar and protect rest, care work, and travel time the model may underestimate.

## 2. Make a decision without pretending uncertainty is gone

**Use when:** you are stuck between a few meaningful options.

```text
Help me think through this decision: [decision].

Options I currently see: [A, B, C]
My goals: [ranked list]
Hard constraints: [money, time, location, commitments]
Known facts: [facts]
Unknowns: [unknowns]
Decision deadline: [date]

Create a decision table using only criteria that affect my goals. Separate facts from my assumptions. For each option, show:
- likely upside and downside
- reversible versus hard-to-reverse consequences
- information that could change the ranking
- a small, low-cost test I could run before committing

End with a provisional recommendation, confidence from 0–100, and the strongest argument against it. Do not manufacture certainty or choose based on generic values I did not provide.
```

**Human check:** ask whether the recommendation reflects your priorities or merely what was easiest to quantify.

## 3. Compare a purchase by total value, not marketing claims

**Use when:** comparing products or services with different prices and trade-offs.

```text
Help me compare these options for [use case]: [links or product details].

My must-haves: [list]
Nice-to-haves: [list]
Budget ceiling: [amount and currency]
Expected years or frequency of use: [value]
Constraints: [compatibility, repairability, privacy, space, accessibility]

Use current primary sources when browsing is available and show the access date. Never infer a specification from marketing language. Return:
1. A fact table with source links and "unknown" where evidence is missing
2. Estimated total cost of ownership, with the formula and assumptions
3. Meaningful differences only
4. Who each option is best and worst for
5. A recommendation plus the condition that would reverse it

Flag sponsored, affiliate, outdated, or unverifiable evidence. Do not invent prices, warranty terms, or availability.
```

**Human check:** verify price, returns, warranty, and compatibility directly with the seller before paying.

## 4. Design a two-week habit experiment

**Use when:** you want to test a behavior change without turning it into a permanent identity project.

```text
Turn this desired change into a two-week experiment: [behavior or outcome].

Why it matters to me: [reason]
Current pattern: [what happens now]
Likely friction: [time, environment, emotion, skill]
Resources and constraints: [details]

Propose one behavior small enough to perform on a difficult day. Define:
- a clear trigger and action
- a two-minute fallback version
- an environment change that reduces friction
- a simple daily measure that does not require an app
- review questions for day 7 and day 14
- stop conditions if the experiment is harmful or disruptive

Avoid motivational slogans and moral judgments. Treat a missed day as data. Offer at most two variants and explain the trade-off.
```

**Human check:** make sure the experiment is safe for your health and circumstances; consult a qualified professional where appropriate.
