# Writing & Content

Prompts for useful, truthful writing that keeps sources and authorship visible. Never publish generated text without editorial review.

## 1. Turn a topic into an evidence-ready article brief

**Use when:** a writer needs a strong assignment before drafting.

```text
Create an article brief.

Topic and audience: [details]
Reader problem or question: [details]
Editorial goal and point of view: [details]
Available sources and subject-matter experts: [list]
Length, channel, and deadline: [details]

Return a working thesis, reader promise, scope and exclusions, outline, evidence needed per section, interview questions, likely counterarguments, and a fact-check list. Distinguish supplied facts from research tasks.

Do not invent statistics, quotations, search intent, or expert consensus.
```

**Human check:** the editor confirms the angle, evidence plan, and any conflicts of interest.

## 2. Draft from a closed set of sources

**Use when:** accuracy matters more than a model's background knowledge.

```text
Draft an article using only the sources below.

Audience, purpose, and length: [details]
Sources with titles and links: [paste]
Required points: [list]
Voice examples: [optional]

Cite each factual claim to a supplied source, distinguish direct evidence from interpretation, and mark gaps [SOURCE NEEDED]. Include a concise introduction, logical sections, counterpoint or limitation, and conclusion. Finish with a claim-to-source table.

Do not add facts from memory, create quotations, or make a source say more than it does.
```

**Human check:** open every cited source and confirm wording, context, dates, and permissions.

## 3. Diagnose structure before line editing

**Use when:** a draft feels slow or confusing but polishing sentences would be premature.

```text
Perform a structural edit on this draft: [paste]

Intended audience and outcome: [details]
Non-negotiable material: [details]
Length target: [target]

Map each section's job, identify the actual thesis, and flag repetition, missing logic, buried context, weak transitions, and unsupported detours. Propose a revised outline and a move/cut/expand table. Preserve the author's substantive position and label any suggested new claim that needs evidence.

Do not rewrite the full draft yet.
```

**Human check:** the author approves structural changes before sentence-level rewriting.

## 4. Rewrite for plain language without losing precision

**Use when:** specialist content must become understandable to a defined audience.

```text
Rewrite this text for [audience] at approximately [reading level or knowledge level]: [paste]

Terms that must stay exact: [list]
Required warnings, caveats, or legal text: [details]
Desired tone and format: [details]

Shorten sentences, define necessary terms, use concrete examples, and surface the main action early. Preserve qualifications and technical meaning. Return the rewrite plus a table of material changes and any passage where simplification may have altered meaning.

Do not turn uncertainty into certainty or remove required caveats.
```

**Human check:** a subject-matter expert and a representative reader review the simplified version.

## 5. Generate headlines without clickbait

**Use when:** a strong piece needs accurate framing for different channels.

```text
Generate headline options for this content: [summary or draft]

Audience and channel: [details]
Primary value or finding: [details]
Words, claims, or tones to avoid: [list]
Length constraints: [details]

Provide 15 options grouped as direct, benefit-led, question-led, and distinctive. For each, note the promise it makes and any risk of ambiguity or overclaiming. Recommend three that best match the actual content and explain why.

Do not use false urgency, unsupported superlatives, or a question the article does not answer.
```

**Human check:** ensure the selected headline is fully delivered by the content.

## 6. Build an FAQ from real questions

**Use when:** users repeatedly need answers spread across documents or teams.

```text
Create a draft FAQ from these real questions and approved sources: [paste]

Audience and context: [details]
Answer owners: [roles]
Topics that require escalation: [list]

Cluster duplicate questions by user intent. Draft a short direct answer first, then details, source link, last-reviewed date placeholder, and escalation path. Identify unanswered questions and conflicting source material.

Do not invent policy or bury material restrictions in vague language.
```

**Human check:** each answer owner verifies accuracy and establishes an update cadence.

## 7. Write a tutorial that anticipates failure

**Use when:** readers need to complete a task, not merely understand a concept.

```text
Draft a task-based tutorial.

Reader starting knowledge: [details]
Target outcome: [outcome]
Verified environment, version, or prerequisites: [details]
Source procedure and examples: [paste]
Known failure cases: [list]

Return prerequisites, a quick success path, numbered steps with observable checkpoints, troubleshooting by symptom, rollback or recovery, and next steps. Mark version-sensitive statements and unverified commands clearly.

Do not claim a step was tested unless evidence is supplied.
```

**Human check:** a person matching the target reader runs the tutorial in the stated environment.

## 8. Shape a presentation around one decision

**Use when:** slides need a narrative rather than a document broken into rectangles.

```text
Create a presentation narrative.

Audience, meeting, and decision: [details]
Time available: [minutes]
Evidence and source notes: [paste]
Known objections or sensitivities: [list]

Propose a one-sentence throughline and a slide-by-slide plan containing purpose, key message, evidence, visual idea, speaker note, and transition. Put supporting detail in an appendix. Include an opening that establishes relevance and an ending with the exact decision or next step.

Do not fabricate charts, data, agreement, or visual evidence.
```

**Human check:** rehearse for timing and verify that every visual represents the underlying data honestly.

## 9. Convert a transcript into accountable notes

**Use when:** a meeting or interview transcript needs decisions and follow-up without invented certainty.

```text
Turn this transcript into structured notes: [paste]

Meeting purpose and participant names or roles: [details]

Return: concise summary, decisions explicitly made, options discussed, unresolved questions, action items with stated owner and date, risks, and notable quotations with speaker and timestamp when available. Mark inferred actions or ambiguous commitments [CONFIRM]. Preserve disagreement and distinguish proposals from decisions.

Do not assign owners, deadlines, or consensus that were not stated.
```

**Human check:** circulate the notes to participants for correction, especially decisions and commitments.

## 10. Adapt content across languages and cultures

**Use when:** meaning and intent matter more than literal translation.

```text
Adapt this content from [source language/locale] to [target language/locale]: [paste]

Audience and channel: [details]
Terms, names, and claims that must remain exact: [list]
Desired tone: [details]
Cultural or regulatory context already known: [details]

Produce a natural target-language version, then list non-literal choices, idioms or examples changed, ambiguous source passages, and items requiring local expertise. Preserve evidence, warnings, and uncertainty. Offer alternatives where register or terminology is contested.

Do not localize legal, medical, financial, or regulated claims without qualified review.
```

**Human check:** a fluent local reviewer with domain knowledge approves the final version.
