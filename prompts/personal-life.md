# Personal Life

Practical prompts for planning and everyday choices. They organize your thinking; they do not make medical, legal, or financial decisions for you.

## 1. Plan a realistic week from actual capacity

**Use when:** your list is longer than your available time and everything feels equally urgent.

```text
Act as a practical planning partner. Help me design a realistic week, not an aspirational one.

My fixed commitments: [days, times, travel]
My available energy by day: [low / medium / high]
Tasks and rough deadlines: [list]
What matters most this week: [one or two outcomes]
What I am willing to postpone: [list or "help me decide"]

First identify missing time costs, dependencies, and tasks that are too vague to schedule. Then produce:
1. A weekly plan with no more than three priority blocks per day
2. Buffer time and one deliberately unscheduled block
3. A "not this week" list with reasons
4. A minimum viable version for any overloaded day
5. The two assumptions most likely to break the plan

Do not optimize every minute. If the commitments cannot fit, say so plainly and propose trade-offs instead of compressing them unrealistically.
```

**Human check:** confirm durations against your calendar and protect rest, care work, and travel time the model may underestimate.

## 2. Make a decision without pretending uncertainty is gone

**Use when:** you are stuck between a few meaningful options.

```text
Help me think through this decision: [decision].

Options I currently see: [A, B, C]
My goals: [ranked list]
Hard constraints: [money, time, location, commitments]
Known facts: [facts]
Unknowns: [unknowns]
Decision deadline: [date]

Create a decision table using only criteria that affect my goals. Separate facts from my assumptions. For each option, show:
- likely upside and downside
- reversible versus hard-to-reverse consequences
- information that could change the ranking
- a small, low-cost test I could run before committing

End with a provisional recommendation, confidence from 0–100, and the strongest argument against it. Do not manufacture certainty or choose based on generic values I did not provide.
```

**Human check:** ask whether the recommendation reflects your priorities or merely what was easiest to quantify.

## 3. Compare a purchase by total value, not marketing claims

**Use when:** comparing products or services with different prices and trade-offs.

```text
Help me compare these options for [use case]: [links or product details].

My must-haves: [list]
Nice-to-haves: [list]
Budget ceiling: [amount and currency]
Expected years or frequency of use: [value]
Constraints: [compatibility, repairability, privacy, space, accessibility]

Use current primary sources when browsing is available and show the access date. Never infer a specification from marketing language. Return:
1. A fact table with source links and "unknown" where evidence is missing
2. Estimated total cost of ownership, with the formula and assumptions
3. Meaningful differences only
4. Who each option is best and worst for
5. A recommendation plus the condition that would reverse it

Flag sponsored, affiliate, outdated, or unverifiable evidence. Do not invent prices, warranty terms, or availability.
```

**Human check:** verify price, returns, warranty, and compatibility directly with the seller before paying.

## 4. Design a two-week habit experiment

**Use when:** you want to test a behavior change without turning it into a permanent identity project.

```text
Turn this desired change into a two-week experiment: [behavior or outcome].

Why it matters to me: [reason]
Current pattern: [what happens now]
Likely friction: [time, environment, emotion, skill]
Resources and constraints: [details]

Propose one behavior small enough to perform on a difficult day. Define:
- a clear trigger and action
- a two-minute fallback version
- an environment change that reduces friction
- a simple daily measure that does not require an app
- review questions for day 7 and day 14
- stop conditions if the experiment is harmful or disruptive

Avoid motivational slogans and moral judgments. Treat a missed day as data. Offer at most two variants and explain the trade-off.
```

**Human check:** make sure the experiment is safe for your health and circumstances; consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

## 5. Plan meals from real constraints

**Use when:** you want a practical food plan without pretending nutrition is one-size-fits-all.

```text
Create a [number]-day meal-planning draft.

People and schedule: [details]
Budget and shopping access: [details]
Foods available or preferred: [list]
Allergies, restrictions, or clinician guidance: [details]
Cooking time, equipment, and skill: [details]

Propose flexible meals, reusable components, a shopping list grouped by section, preparation order, storage notes, and low-energy alternatives. Minimize waste and state assumptions. Do not diagnose, prescribe a diet, or override medical advice.
```

**Human check:** verify allergies, food safety, health needs, prices, and what the household will realistically eat.

## 6. Build a travel plan with uncertainty visible

**Use when:** arranging a trip whose schedules, prices, or entry rules may change.

```text
Draft a travel plan for [destination and dates].

Travelers and accessibility needs: [details]
Budget and pace: [details]
Confirmed bookings: [details]
Interests and non-goals: [list]
Sources checked and their dates: [links]

Create a day-by-day outline with travel time, rest margin, booking dependencies, estimated cost ranges, weather alternatives, and cancellation checkpoints. Separate confirmed facts, current web findings, and suggestions. Mark entry, visa, health, and local-rule information for official verification.
```

**Human check:** confirm all critical details with official providers and authorities shortly before travel.

## 7. Triage a home-maintenance problem safely

**Use when:** you need to describe and contain a household issue before calling the right professional.

```text
Help me prepare a safe maintenance triage, not a repair diagnosis.

Observed problem: [what, where, when]
Changes over time: [details]
Photos, sounds, smells, readings, or error codes: [details]
Building and equipment context: [details]
Actions already taken: [list]

Organize observations, immediate safety red flags, reversible containment steps, information to collect, and questions for the appropriate professional. Suggest possible categories only as hypotheses. For gas, fire, structural, electrical, water-contamination, or carbon-monoxide risk, prioritize leaving the area and contacting emergency or qualified services.
```

**Human check:** do not open, energize, dismantle, or repair anything beyond your competence and local rules.

## 8. Prepare a family coordination conversation

**Use when:** responsibilities are unclear and resentment is replacing explicit agreements.

```text
Create a neutral agenda for a family or household coordination conversation.

People and relevant needs: [details]
Recurring responsibilities: [list]
Current friction described without blame: [details]
Availability, capacity, and constraints: [details]

Return a short opening, shared facts, questions about capacity and fairness, a visible responsibility map, options for rotation or backup, and a two-week trial with a review date. Distinguish equal effort from equitable contribution. Do not assign motives or decide for absent people.
```

**Human check:** let each person correct the facts and consent to the experiment; prioritize safety in coercive situations.

## 9. Create a personal digital-privacy cleanup plan

**Use when:** accounts and devices have accumulated without a clear security routine.

```text
Build a prioritized digital-privacy cleanup checklist.

Devices and major account types: [list]
Password manager and multifactor status: [details]
Known breaches, suspicious activity, or lost devices: [details]
Threats I reasonably care about: [details]
Time available: [time]

Group actions into urgent, this week, and later. Cover recovery contacts, unique passwords, MFA, updates, permissions, backups, data-sharing settings, old accounts, and scam resilience. Explain the benefit and lockout risk of each action. Never request passwords, recovery codes, private keys, or identity documents.
```

**Human check:** use official account pages, preserve recovery access, and consult a security specialist after compromise or targeted abuse.

## 10. Clear a personal administration backlog

**Use when:** forms, renewals, bills, and appointments feel like one undifferentiated burden.

```text
Turn this personal-admin backlog into a realistic action queue: [paste]

Deadlines and consequences I know: [details]
Available time and support: [details]
Items involving money, health, legal rights, or government services: [list]

Classify items by deadline, consequence, effort, dependency, and emotional friction. Propose a first 30-minute session, a weekly sequence, documents to gather, calls or questions to prepare, and reminders. Mark all inferred deadlines [VERIFY] and do not give legal, medical, or financial conclusions.
```

**Human check:** confirm deadlines and requirements with the responsible institution before relying on the plan.
