# Communication & Decisions

Prompts for clearer messages and more accountable decisions.

## 1. Draft a sensitive message that stays direct

**Use when:** you need to communicate a boundary, problem, delay, or refusal.

```text
Help me draft a sensitive message.

Recipient and relationship: [details]
What happened, stated as observable facts: [facts]
What I need them to understand: [point]
What I am asking for or declining: [request or boundary]
Tone: [warm / neutral / formal]
Channel and length: [email, chat, conversation notes]
Words or implications to avoid: [details]

Write three versions: concise, warm, and formal. Each must distinguish fact from interpretation, state the request clearly, and leave no fake ambiguity. Do not add blame, diagnoses, threats, legal claims, or promises I did not provide.

After the drafts, identify the sentence most likely to be misread and offer a safer alternative.
```

**Human check:** read it from the recipient's position and confirm the request, deadline, and consequences are accurate.

## 2. Write a decision memo with a visible dissent path

**Use when:** a group needs a durable record of why a choice was made.

```text
Turn the material below into a decision memo.

Decision: [question]
Decision owner: [role]
Deadline: [date]
Options considered: [list]
Evidence: [paste facts, links, analysis]
Constraints: [list]
Stakeholders affected: [list]

Return: context, decision criteria, options with trade-offs, recommended option, rationale linked to evidence, risks and mitigations, unresolved unknowns, dissenting view, and review date. Include a short "what would make us reverse this?" section.

Do not hide uncertainty behind executive language. Mark unsupported statements and do not invent stakeholder agreement.
```

**Human check:** ask the accountable owner and one affected dissenter to review the memo before treating it as final.

## 3. Map a disagreement before trying to solve it

**Use when:** people repeat positions but may disagree about different things.

```text
Analyze this disagreement without choosing a winner yet.

Statements from each side: [paste or summarize accurately]
Shared goal, if known: [goal]
Decision that must be made: [decision]
Time or policy constraints: [constraints]

Build a map with:
1. Claims each side is making
2. Evidence each claim relies on
3. Values or priorities underneath the claims
4. Terms that may have different meanings
5. Facts the sides appear to agree on
6. Testable disagreements versus value trade-offs

Propose three neutral questions that could reduce uncertainty and one low-risk experiment. Steelman each side in language they would likely recognize. Do not infer hidden motives.
```

**Human check:** let each side correct its own representation before moving to solutions.

## 4. Prepare a negotiation with walk-away clarity

**Use when:** negotiating scope, pay, price, timeline, or responsibilities.

```text
Help me prepare for a negotiation about [topic].

My interests, ranked: [list]
Likely interests of the other side, clearly labeled as assumptions: [list]
Objective facts or benchmarks I have: [facts and sources]
My alternatives if there is no agreement: [BATNA]
My boundaries and authority: [details]
Relationship considerations: [details]

Create a preparation sheet with: opening frame, questions to discover interests, tradable variables, three packages rather than one demand, objective criteria, likely objections, and a clear walk-away condition. Draft language that is firm without implying threats.

Flag where current market, legal, contractual, or financial information requires verification. Do not invent leverage or recommend deception.
```

**Human check:** verify benchmarks and authority; seek specialist advice for high-stakes employment, legal, or financial terms.

## 5. Write an apology that owns the impact

**Use when:** a mistake needs acknowledgment without excuses, performance, or pressure for forgiveness.

```text
Draft an apology from these facts: [paste]

Person or group affected: [details]
Impact I understand: [details]
Responsibility I can honestly own: [details]
Repair already possible: [details]

Write a concise version that names the action, acknowledges impact, takes proportionate responsibility, avoids intent as a defense, and offers a specific repair or changed behavior. Do not request reassurance or forgiveness. Flag facts or impact I should confirm rather than assume.
```

**Human check:** deliver it in a safe, appropriate channel and follow through on repair without demanding a response.

## 6. Request a refund, correction, or cancellation

**Use when:** a service problem needs a factual and actionable written request.

```text
Draft a clear request using only these records: [paste]

Desired remedy: [details]
Order, account, or case references: [details]
Relevant published terms: [quote or link]
Deadline and channel: [details]

Create a subject line, short chronology, evidence list, requested remedy, reasonable response date, and escalation question. Keep firm and neutral. Redact unnecessary personal data and distinguish documented terms from my interpretation.

Do not invent consumer rights, deadlines, threats, or legal conclusions.
```

**Human check:** verify the recipient, policy, jurisdiction, attachments, and any formal deadline before sending.

## 7. Give feedback about observable work

**Use when:** feedback should help someone act without speculating about personality or motive.

```text
Help me prepare feedback.

Relationship and context: [details]
Observed behavior or work product: [specific facts]
Impact and evidence: [details]
Expected standard or shared goal: [source]
What I want to understand: [questions]

Draft a conversational version separating observation, impact, question, and request. Include space for missing context and a jointly defined next step. Remove labels, mind-reading, absolutes, and feedback unrelated to the person's role.
```

**Human check:** verify your examples, account for power and bias, and choose a setting where dialogue is possible.

## 8. Facilitate a difficult conversation

**Use when:** people need a structured way to discuss conflict without forcing artificial agreement.

```text
Design a facilitation plan for this conversation: [details]

Participants and power relationships: [details]
Shared decision or outcome: [details]
Known facts, contested interpretations, and harms: [details]
Safety, confidentiality, or policy constraints: [details]

Propose an opening agreement, balanced speaking process, questions, fact/interpretation parking lot, options for pause, and decision or follow-up method. Preserve dissent and identify when a neutral professional or formal process is more appropriate.

Do not mediate abuse, retaliation, discrimination, or legal disputes as if they were ordinary misunderstandings.
```

**Human check:** confirm consent, safety, authority, and escalation routes before convening the discussion.

## 9. Turn detail into an executive summary

**Use when:** a decision-maker needs the essential message without losing uncertainty.

```text
Create an executive summary from this material: [paste]

Audience and decision: [details]
Length limit: [limit]
Required facts, risks, and dissent: [list]

Lead with the decision or implication, then cover evidence, options, trade-offs, recommendation, risks, unknowns, and requested action. Link each quantitative claim to its source. Put supporting detail in an appendix and retain material bad news.
```

**Human check:** compare the summary with the source and confirm that compression did not hide a critical limitation.

## 10. Draft a stakeholder update with no surprises

**Use when:** progress, delay, or changed scope needs transparent communication.

```text
Draft a stakeholder update.

Original commitment and date: [details]
Current status with evidence: [details]
What changed and why: [facts]
Impact on scope, timing, cost, quality, or people: [details]
Actions, owners, and decisions needed: [details]

Return a short headline, progress since last update, variance, impact, recovery or replan options, risks, and explicit asks. Separate causes supported by evidence from hypotheses. State what is unchanged and when the next update will occur.

Do not conceal uncertainty, blame individuals, or promise dates without owner confirmation.
```

**Human check:** owners verify status and commitments before distribution.
